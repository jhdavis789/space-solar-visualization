What "Atmosphere" toggles
When ON, sunlight passing through Earth's atmosphere is attenuated before reaching the panel. When OFF, the only loss is geometric shadowing (night/eclipse).
Ground panel model
Air mass via Kasten-Young (1989):
AM = 1/(sin(α) + 0.50572·(α°+6.08)^-1.6364)
where α = solar elevation angle. At zenith AM=1, at horizon AM≈38.

Transmittance via Meinel (1976) clear-sky broadband:
T = 0.7^(AM^0.678)
The 0.678 exponent averages Rayleigh scattering, Mie aerosol, and molecular absorption across the full solar spectrum.

Clear sky only — no clouds, rain, or haze. Real-world ground output is ~25-50% lower depending on climate.
Space panel model
Near the shadow boundary, the satellite-to-Sun line of sight grazes Earth's atmosphere. The tangent height ht is the closest approach of that ray to Earth's surface.

Limb air mass via Chapman function for exponential atmosphere (ρ = ρ₀·e^(-h/H)):
AM(h_t) = e^(-h_t/H) · √(2π(R_e+h_t)/H)
H = 8.5 km (scale height). This is the exact analytical integral of density along the chord.

Same Meinel transmittance: T = 0.7^(AM^0.678)

Effect is small (~0.01-0.1% annual average) since the penumbra transition spans only ~80 km of tangent height (~12 seconds of orbit).
What "Weather" toggles
When ON, ground panel output is reduced by monthly cloud/weather factors for Los Angeles based on TMY3 (Typical Meteorological Year) measured data.

Monthly factors (fraction of clear-sky GHI reaching ground):
Jan 0.70 · Feb 0.72 · Mar 0.70 · Apr 0.74
May 0.70 · Jun 0.78 · Jul 0.87 · Aug 0.86
Sep 0.82 · Oct 0.75 · Nov 0.74 · Dec 0.69

Annual average: ~0.76 (LA is one of the sunniest US cities).
Accounts for: clouds, marine layer, haze, fog, and aerosols.

Space panel is unaffected — no weather in orbit.
Note: These data centers may not need 24/7 operation. Even a few hours/year of compute time could suffice for some workloads, making the energy economics even more favorable for space.
